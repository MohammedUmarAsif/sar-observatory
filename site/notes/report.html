Question

How do controlled platform-velocity mismatch and residual linear Doppler phase affect point-target quality, and which automated diagnostics distinguish blur from displacement? Engineering relevance: product validation needs independent checks of impulse shape, geometric location and coherent response.

Methods

One isolated point target, rectangular LFM chirp and finite aperture, flat Cartesian geometry and stop-and-go sampling. Delays and two-way carrier phase are exact within the model. Linear matched filtering is followed by direct time-domain backprojection. Complex compressed samples are interpolated linearly; pulses are coherently averaged. Main cases change one factor. An extended scene is separate so neighboring responses do not contaminate isolated-target metrics.

Independent checks compare FFT filtering to direct convolution, backprojection to a raw-echo matched inner product, and widths/sidelobes to analytic references. Half-power crossings use linear power interpolation; sidelobe energy is measured outside first local minima in x ±8 m and y ±30 m windows. Discretization allowances are 0.10 m and 1.0 m for the respective full widths, not statistical error bars.

First-minimum discretization, finite chirp duration and interpolation differ from ideal infinite sinc conventions.

Residual +4 Hz Doppler leaves width at 0.2947 m, but moves the peak 0.850 m from its truth position. The width-only gate accepts this case; the combined gate rejects it. Velocity errors broaden responses and reduce coherent peak power. At severe mismatch, the brightest lobe can move, so blur and localization are not strictly separable. Halving aperture or bandwidth produces the corresponding resolution degradation.

Six independent seeded −25 dB record-SNR trials give mean azimuth width 0.29728 m, sample SD 0.00271 m, and empirical range 0.29223 to 0.29991 m. All 6 pass the selected gates. This negative finding illustrates coherent averaging: low record SNR alone does not necessarily invalidate the focused point product. These are six trials, not a population interval or a detector ROC study.

Discussion and limitations

The experiment supports combining geometric and impulse-shape diagnostics: sharpness does not establish geometric validity. Real imagery illustrates the parallel inference problem: adaptive class separation does not establish stable temporal water mapping. The two branches share disciplined validation, not one numerical processor or mission configuration.

The simulator omits Earth curvature, antenna gains, path-loss/radiometric calibration, atmosphere, motion during propagation and instrument transfer functions. It does not ingest spaceborne raw formats. Linear interpolation and discrete windows limit precision. Quality gates are educational choices, not mission requirements. The real study has one fixed AOI, no contemporaneous independent labels, no measured subpixel registration accuracy and no locally validated terrain/noise masks. Season, wind, vegetation and other dark surfaces complicate candidate-water inference. Otsu is not demonstrated as an improvement; spatial dependence remains after blocking.

Next justified steps: compatible reference labels and geographic holdouts; independent co-registration checks; a controlled apodization study; a validated fractional-delay interpolator and precision/runtime tradeoff. Avoid deep learning until data and evaluation design actually justify it.
